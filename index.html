A biofeedback racing experience

slowfaster is a minimalist Apple Watch experience where your inner state shapes the world around you. There are no controls. No high scores. No competition. The game doesn't respond to your actions — it responds to you.

How it works

Your car drives automatically through an endless procedurally generated road. The twist? The road's difficulty is controlled by your heart rate delta — the difference between your current heart rate and your personal resting rate.

  • Δ

    Delta measurement

    We calculate the difference between your current heart rate and your resting heart rate (derived from your sleep data).

  • ↗

    Elevated state

    When your heart rate is elevated, the road becomes winding with tight curves and frequent direction changes.

  • →

    Calm state

    As you approach your resting rate, the road straightens out. Speed feels faster. Flow emerges.

  • ◎

    No explicit goal

    There's no score, no game over, no finish line. The reward is the state itself.

The science behind it

slowfaster is built on decades of research in heart rate variability (HRV) biofeedback — a scientifically validated approach for stress reduction and autonomic regulation.

Heart Rate Variability Biofeedback: How and Why Does It Work?

Frontiers in Psychology, 2014

This comprehensive review demonstrates that HRV biofeedback produces improvements in depression, anxiety, and stress management. The mechanism involves stimulating the parasympathetic nervous system through rhythmic activity.

Read the study →

A Healthy Heart Is Not a Metronome

Integrative Physiological & Behavioral Science, 1998

Landmark paper establishing that heart rate variability reflects the dynamic interplay between sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems. Higher variability indicates better stress resilience and emotional regulation capacity.

View on PubMed →

The Effects of Biofeedback Training on Stress and Anxiety

Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback, 2020

Meta-analysis of 58 studies showing significant effects of biofeedback interventions on reducing physiological and psychological symptoms of stress. Real-time feedback enhances awareness and voluntary control of autonomic functions.

Read on Springer →

Resonance Frequency Biofeedback Training

Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback, 2013

Research on how biofeedback at one's resonance frequency (typically around 6 breaths/minute) maximizes HRV amplitude and strengthens baroreflex function, leading to improved emotional regulation and stress resilience.

View on PubMed →
